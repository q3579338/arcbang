Where the universe comes from

The hash is unpacked into 23 numbers, all of them physical constants: how strong gravity is, how heavy the electron is, how much dark energy there is, how many dimensions space has. Those 23 numbers become the starting conditions, and real cosmology takes it from there — inflation through to heat death.

So it is a function, not a random generator. The same hash gives the same universe today and in ten years. I cannot nudge one into looking nicer either; the hash says what it says.

Most are duds

The first thing that usually goes wrong is D. D is the number of dimensions space has — we live with 3. Hashes hand out 9, 14, 18 all the time. Gravity gets too steep at those numbers, planets cannot hold an orbit and electrons cannot hold onto a nucleus, so the whole thing is over in the first second.

Clear that and there is another gate, then another: atoms, galaxies, stars, carbon. The run stops at the first one it fails and that failure becomes the name of the universe. I drew 1,600 random blocks once; more than nine in ten stopped at the very first gate.

Once in a while, one works

Every fifteen to thirty blocks or so you hit a good one: galaxies form, stars light, there is carbon and water, and there is time to spare. Those the engine will draw all the way down to a planet surface, and you can turn it around and look.

How to play

Open the simulator and hit "latest", or type in a height you care about — the block from your birthday, the block your first transaction landed in. A few seconds and you have a universe. No money, no wallet, look at as many as you like.

There is also a sandbox where you push the constants around yourself and watch the ending move. Purely for fun; nothing you make there touches the chain and none of it can be minted.

1,387 of them

The universe is 13.787 billion years old, so that is the number I took: one NFT for every ten million years. When they are gone they are gone, and I will not add more.

The first 387 are free, one per address, you only pay gas. Gas on Arc is USDC, which works out to a fraction of a cent per mint, so you never need to keep some separate fuel token around. After that a mint is 1 USDC and any one address can take at most 3 more. The cap of 3 is there so a script cannot walk off with the lot, not to make anybody feel rushed.

What you get is an ordinary ERC-721. List it on OpenSea whenever you like; royalties are 5%.

The code

The engine is open source under AGPL-3.0. How the 23 constants are derived and how an ending is decided is clearer in the code than in a page of tables I could write here: github.com/q3579338/arcbang.

If you want to check that a particular NFT really came out of its block hash, verify has two commands for it. Takes a couple of minutes.
